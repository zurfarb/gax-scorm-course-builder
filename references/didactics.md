# Didactic guidelines

How to turn raw material into a course that changes what learners can do. These are defaults based on common e-learning practice; a brief or the user can override them for a course.

## 1. Principle: start from the objectives

Every element of the course must serve a learning objective (constructive alignment):

```
objective  →  content that enables it  →  activity that practises it  →  question that proves it
```

If a piece of content serves no objective, leave it out, even if the source material spends pages on it. If an objective has no activity or no quiz question, the course cannot show that it was reached. `validate.py` warns about objectives without chapter or quiz coverage.

## 2. Writing learning objectives

- 2-6 objectives per course. More means the course is too big: suggest splitting it.
- Each objective describes an **observable** outcome, from the learner's point of view, starting with "Du kannst …" / "You can …".
- Avoid verbs that cannot be observed: *wissen, verstehen, kennen, sich bewusst sein / know, understand, be aware of*.
- Assign a Bloom level. It decides which elements and question types fit (section 5 and `elements.md`).

| Level (label in concept.md) | Meaning | de-CH verbs | en-GB verbs | Fitting activities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `remember` (erinnern) | recall facts, terms | nennen, aufzählen, wiedergeben | name, list, recall | flipcards, single/multiple choice |
| `understand` (verstehen) | explain, distinguish | erklären, unterscheiden, zuordnen, begründen | explain, distinguish, match | tabs (comparison), matching, true/false with explanation |
| `apply` (anwenden) | use in a concrete situation | anwenden, durchführen, erstellen, entscheiden | apply, carry out, create, decide | steps, scenario, scenario-based quiz questions |
| `analyse` (analysieren) | examine a situation, find causes | analysieren, prüfen, erkennen, beurteilen | analyse, examine, identify, assess | scenario with several plausible choices, case questions |

Most corporate courses should have at least one `apply` objective. A course of pure `remember` objectives is usually a reference document, not training; point this out in the concept.

## 3. Selecting content

Sort the material into three groups and note the result in `concept.md`:

- **Need to know:** required to reach an objective → goes on the pages.
- **Nice to know:** useful background → accordion, or leave out.
- **Not needed:** history, organisational details, repetitions, legal texts in full → leave out, or link to the source if learners need it later.

Rules of thumb:
- Condense, do not copy. A 30-page PDF typically becomes 6-12 course pages.
- Prefer examples from the learners' daily work over abstract definitions.
- One page explains one idea. If you need "and also" on a page, it is two pages.

## 4. Structuring chapters and pages

- **Chapter:** usually one objective (or two closely related ones), 2-5 pages. Give the first page an `intro` that says why the chapter matters.
- **Page:** one key message, a title that states it ("Länge schlägt Komplexität" is better than "Passwortlänge"), 80-200 words of visible text. `lint_text.py` warns above 250 words.
- **Order within a chapter:** why it matters → explanation → example → activity → summary.
- The **start page** (title, description, objectives, duration) and the **end page** are generated by the player. Do not create pages for them.
- End each chapter, or at least the course, with a `summary` block of 3-5 key takeaways phrased as statements.
- **Navigation:** `free` (default) suits experienced learners and reference-like courses: they can jump to what they need. Choose `sequential` only when chapters build strictly on each other (e.g. a process taught step by step) or the brief demands a fixed order; it unlocks a chapter once all pages of the previous one were visited.

## 5. Activation

Learners remember what they do, not what they read.

- Put an activity on at least every second or third page: `check`, `scenario`, `flipcards`, or an interactive element (`tabs`, `accordion`, `steps`) if the content suits it.
- Each chapter has at least one `check` or `scenario`. Use a `scenario` for every `apply`/`analyse` objective.
- Knowledge checks are formative: they give immediate feedback and do not count. Do not reuse them as final quiz questions; ask about the same objective from a different angle.

## 6. Assessment

### Final quiz

- At least **2 questions per objective**, 6-12 questions in total. Questions are shuffled and solutions are shown after submitting, so the quiz is also a learning tool.
- Match the question type to the Bloom level (table in section 2). For `apply`, write short situations ("Du erhältst … Was tust du?") instead of definition questions.
- Set `objective` on every question; `validate.py` uses it for coverage.

### Writing good questions

- The prompt alone must make sense; the learner should be able to answer before reading the options.
- 3-4 options for single choice, 4-5 for multiple choice. Every distractor must be plausible, ideally a typical misconception.
- Options of similar length and style; the correct one must not stand out by being longer or more precise.
- No "alle Antworten sind richtig / all of the above", no "keine der Antworten / none of the above".
- No negations in the prompt; if unavoidable, bold them (**nicht**).
- No trick questions, no questions about details that do not serve an objective.
- True/false statements must be unambiguously true or false, not "mostly true".

### Feedback

- `feedback.correct` confirms *why* it is right; `feedback.incorrect` gives a hint without simply revealing the answer. The player already shows "Richtig!" / "Leider nicht ganz." as heading, so do not start the feedback text with those words.
- `explanation` (shown with the solution) explains the reasoning in 1-2 sentences.

## 7. Duration

`validate.py` computes the estimate (`scripts/gaxlib/duration.py`) and warns if `durationMinutes` differs by more than 5 minutes; `build.py` prints it. The formula:

- reading: 1 minute per 180 words the learner actually reads: all page texts including accordions and flip cards; for knowledge checks the prompt, options, the longer of the two feedback texts and the explanation; for scenarios the situation, choices and the two longest feedback texts
- plus 0.5 minute per knowledge check, 1.5 minutes per scenario, 0.5 minute per interactive block (tabs, steps, flipcards, accordion)
- plus 0.5 minute per final quiz question

Set `durationMinutes` to the estimate rounded to the nearest 5 minutes (22.4 → 20, 22.5 → 25).

### Word budget at concept time

Real texts are longer than planned, mostly because of feedback texts. Plan with these budgets and add them up in the concept:

| Element | Words |
|---|---|
| page text (text, text-image, callout, tabs, steps …) | 80-200 per page |
| knowledge check (prompt, options, feedback, explanation) | 60-80 |
| scenario (situation, 3 choices, 2 feedbacks read) | 150-200 |
| summary | 30-50 |

At 180 words per minute, a 20-minute course with 8 quiz questions has room for roughly 2000 words in total, including the time for activities.

Typical targets: micro-learning 5-10 minutes, standard course 15-30 minutes. Above 30 minutes, suggest splitting into several courses (one SCORM package each), because learners rarely finish long modules in one go.

### When the target duration and the minimums collide

The user's target duration wins over the guidelines in this file. If the estimate is above the target, cut in this order and say so in the concept:

1. fewer objectives (merge or move one to another course); each objective removed saves its pages, activities and 2 quiz questions
2. nice-to-know content (accordions, extra examples)
3. fewer pages per chapter, by condensing text
4. only then: 1 quiz question for `remember`/`understand` objectives, and one shared summary at the end instead of one per chapter

Keep at least one activity per chapter and one scenario per `apply`/`analyse` objective: they are what makes the course training rather than reading. If the target still cannot be met, show both variants in the concept (target length vs. recommended length) and let the user choose.

## 8. Tone and accessibility

- Address the learner directly (de-CH: "du"; en-GB: "you"). Active voice, short sentences, concrete verbs.
- Emphasise with **bold**, sparingly (at most 1-2 per paragraph). Never rely on colour alone.
- Every informative image has an `alt` text describing its content and purpose ("Diagramm: Ein Passwort mit 16 Zeichen braucht …"), not its appearance ("blaues Bild"). Decorative images get an empty `alt`, but avoid purely decorative images.
- Link texts say where they lead ("Datenschutzrichtlinie (Intranet)"), never "hier klicken".
- Do not put essential information only inside images; repeat it in text.

## 9. Working with source material

- All factual statements must be traceable to the input or the user. Do not invent figures, legal requirements, internal processes, contacts or URLs.
- If the material is contradictory, outdated or incomplete, list it under "Offene Fragen" in `concept.md` and ask at approval 1, instead of guessing. Until the answer comes, plan with the most plausible reading and say which one.
- If a source document states a decision that resolves a contradiction elsewhere (e.g. a dated decision log), follow the decision and mention it.
- Information that exists only inside an image (labels, markers, abbreviations) must not be guessed: ask what it means, or leave it out of the text.
- Examples and scenarios may be invented, as long as they are realistic and do not contradict the material.
- Keep company-specific terms, product names and process names exactly as in the source.

### Exports from Obsidian, Confluence or wikis

Input is often exported from a knowledge base. Handle it like this:

- **Frontmatter** (`title`, `status`, `quelle`, `tags` …) is metadata, not course content. Use it to judge reliability: `status: entwurf` or a synthesis/summary document is less certain than an official source page; mark statements that rely only on such documents in the concept.
- **Links to missing pages** (`[[Page]]`) and **missing embedded images** (`![[image.png]]`) are gaps. List the relevant ones under "Offene Fragen" (what is missing and what it would add); ignore links that are clearly out of scope.
- **Export artefacts** (repeated blocks, placeholders such as "Type or paste something here", empty headings) are ignored. Mention them in one line in the concept so the source owner can fix them.
- External links in the material (Confluence, SharePoint, forms) may be used in the course if they are meant for the learners; never link to personal drafts or internal working files.
